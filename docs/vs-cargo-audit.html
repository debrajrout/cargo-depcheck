cargo-audit vs cargo-outdated vs cargo-deny vs cargo-depcheck

These tools are not competitors so much as answers to four different questions. Knowing which question you are asking is most of the choice.

ToolThe question it answersTypical use
cargo-audit Is anything in my tree known to be vulnerable? Block a merge on a published CVE
cargo-outdated What is behind its latest release? See what could be bumped
cargo-deny Does my tree comply with our policy? Enforce licenses, bans, sources, duplicates
cargo-depcheck Which dependency deserves attention first? Decide where limited maintenance time goes

Why “ranked” is the difference that matters

Run cargo outdated on a mature project and you get a list. Forty crates are behind. Which one do you fix on a Tuesday afternoon with an hour to spare? The list does not say, because a flat list cannot: it has no notion of how much of your project rests on each entry.

depcheck scores each crate and multiplies by graph impact, so a crate one breaking release behind that half your tree depends on outranks a leaf five releases behind that nothing imports. That single multiplier is what turns an inventory into an order of work — the scoring reference shows exactly how it is computed, and why it is absolute rather than relative to your project.

Is cargo-depcheck a cargo-audit replacement?

No, and it should not be treated as one. cargo-audit is the right tool to block a merge on a known vulnerability: it is focused, widely trusted, and maintained by the RustSec team who curate the advisory database. depcheck reads that same database, but its job is prioritization, which is a judgment call expressed as a score rather than a yes/no gate.

A finding in depcheck also does not mean you are exploitable — there is no reachability analysis, so it cannot tell whether you call the affected function. It means the crate is in your tree and something about it deserves a look.

Using them together

They compose cleanly, and none of them need to be the only one in your CI: 

# block on known vulnerabilities
cargo audit

# enforce license and source policy
cargo deny check

# rank what to spend maintenance time on, failing only on regressions
cargo depcheck --baseline depcheck-baseline.json --fail-on critical

In GitHub Actions, the depcheck step can post the ranked report as a pull request comment while audit and deny keep their pass/fail role: 

- uses: debrajrout/cargo-depcheck@v1
  with:
    comment: true
    top: 10
    fail-on: critical
